Why Sports Bras Fit Differently

Sports bras are designed to minimize breast movement during physical activity. Unlike everyday bras, they use compression (flattening) or encapsulation (separate cups) to reduce bounce. Sports bras also run tighter in the band – you may need to size up in the cup or band depending on the style and activity level.

Key difference: Your sports bra size might be different from your everyday bra size. Many women wear a smaller band and larger cup in sports bras for better support.

Choose the Right Support Level

🧘 Low Impact

Yoga, walking, pilates, stretching. Light compression, comfortable fit. Look for seamless, wire-free styles.

Tip: Stick to your regular size for low impact.

🚴 Medium Impact

Cycling, gym, hiking, dance. Moderate compression or encapsulation. Look for adjustable straps and a wider band.

Tip: Size down in the band for extra support.

🏃 High Impact

Running, jumping, HIIT, plyometrics. Maximum support with encapsulation or firm compression. Look for racerback styles and moisture-wicking fabric.

Tip: You may need to size up in the cup for high impact.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I size up or down for a sports bra?

Sports bras typically run tighter in the band for support. Many women size down in the band and up in the cup – especially for high impact activities. Always check the brand's size guide.

What's the difference between compression and encapsulation?

Compression bras flatten the breasts against the chest – good for smaller busts. Encapsulation bras have separate cups that support each breast individually – better for larger busts and high impact.

Can I wear a sports bra for everyday use?

You can, but sports bras are designed for movement and may not provide the same shaping as everyday bras. For daily wear, choose a low-impact style for comfort.
