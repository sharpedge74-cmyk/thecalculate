Why Bra Sizes Differ Around the World

Different countries use different sizing conventions. The band number might be the same, but the cup letters can change dramatically from one system to another.

Here's the simple breakdown:

  • US sizing – uses bands in inches (32, 34, 36...) and cup letters A, B, C, D, DD, DDD, G, H, I, J...
  • UK sizing – uses the same bands as US but different cup letters from DD upward: DD, E, F, FF, G, GG, H, HH, J...
  • EU sizing – uses bands in centimeters (70, 75, 80...) and cups A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J...
  • AU / NZ sizing – follows UK cup letters but band numbers are different (e.g., US 34 = AU 12)
  • FR / ES sizing – uses EU bands + 15 (e.g., EU 75 = FR 90) with similar cup letters to EU

The good news? US and UK band numbers are the same. A 34 is a 34 in both systems. The difference is in the cup letters from DD upward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between US and UK bra sizes?

US and UK bands use the same inch numbers (e.g., 34 is 34 in both). However, cup letters differ from DD upward. US DDD = UK E, US G = UK F, US H = UK FF.

How do I convert EU band size to US?

EU bands are measured in centimeters. Subtract 4 from the US band and multiply by 2.54 to get the EU band. For example, US 34 → (34-4) × 2.54 = 76 cm → EU 75.

What are sister sizes?

Sister sizes have the same cup volume but different band and cup letter combinations. If you go down one band size, go up one cup size. If you go up one band size, go down one cup size. For example, 34C → 32D (tighter band) or 36B (looser band).

How is cup size calculated?

Cup size is the difference between your bust measurement and your band measurement. Each inch of difference equals one cup letter: 1" = A, 2" = B, 3" = C, 4" = D, 5" = DD, 6" = DDD, 7" = G, and so on.

Why is my size different in different brands?

Different brands use different fit models, fabric, and construction. Your size may vary by brand – that's why sister sizing and trying on multiple sizes is recommended.
