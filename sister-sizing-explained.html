What Are Sister Sizes?

Sister sizes are bra sizes that have the same cup volume but different band sizes and cup letters. The cup volume stays exactly the same – only the band and cup letter change.

For example, 34C, 32D, and 36B are all sister sizes. They all hold the same amount of breast tissue, but the band is different:

  • 34C – your standard size
  • 32D – smaller band, larger cup (sister size down)
  • 36B – larger band, smaller cup (sister size up)

The rule is simple: Band – 2 = Cup + 1 (tighter band, bigger cup) and Band + 2 = Cup – 1 (looser band, smaller cup).

When to Use Sister Sizes

There are several situations where sister sizing can save the day:

  • Your size is sold out – Sister sizes give you more options to find a bra that fits.
  • Different brands fit differently – Some brands run small or large in the band. A sister size may fit better in a specific brand.
  • The band feels too tight or too loose – If the band is uncomfortable but the cup fits, try a sister size.
  • You're between band sizes – If you're between a 34 and 36 band, sister sizes offer a middle ground.
  • During pregnancy or nursing – Your body changes, and sister sizes give you flexibility.

How to Calculate Sister Sizes

The rule is simple:

Band – 2 = Cup + 1 (tighter band, bigger cup)

Band + 2 = Cup – 1 (looser band, smaller cup)

Example:

  • Your size: 34C
  • Sister size (tighter band): 34 – 2 = 32, C + 1 = D → 32D
  • Sister size (looser band): 34 + 2 = 36, C – 1 = B → 36B

When Sister Sizing Doesn't Work

While sister sizing is helpful, it's not always the perfect solution. Here are some situations where sister sizing might not work:

  • If the cup shape is different – Sister sizes maintain volume but not shape. Some styles may not work with the different band/cup combination.
  • If you go beyond 2 band sizes – Generally, stick to within 1-2 band sizes of your original. Going too far changes the fit significantly.
  • If the bra is a specific style – Some styles like push-ups or sports bras fit differently and may not sister-size well.
  • If you have a very small or very large cup – In extreme cup sizes, sister sizing may not give a good fit.

Tips for Sister Sizing Success

  • Always try on sister sizes – Don't assume they'll fit the same. Try them on to see which feels best.
  • Check the band – Your band should fit firmly on the loosest hook when new.
  • Check the cups – Make sure there's no spillage or gaping in the cups.
  • Check the gore – The center piece should lie flat against your chest.
  • Check the straps – Straps should be comfortable and not dig in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are sister sizes?

Sister sizes are bra sizes that have the same cup volume but different band and cup letter combinations. For example, 34C → 32D (tighter band) or 36B (looser band).

How do I find my sister size?

Use the rule: Band – 2 = Cup + 1 (tighter band, bigger cup) or Band + 2 = Cup – 1 (looser band, smaller cup). For example, 34C → 32D (tighter) or 36B (looser).

Are sister sizes exactly the same?

Sister sizes have the same cup volume but the fit may feel different because the band is different. Try them on to see which feels best for your body.

Can I use sister sizing for any bra style?

Sister sizing works best for standard underwire bras. Sports bras, push-ups, and strapless styles may fit differently – always try them on.

How many sister sizes can I go?

Generally, you should stay within 1-2 sister sizes of your original size. Going too far changes the fit significantly.
