What Is a Cup Size?

Your cup letter (A, B, C, D, etc.) shows the difference between your bust and your band. The bigger the difference, the larger the cup. For example, a 1‑inch difference is an A cup, 2 inches is B, 3 inches is C, and so on.

But cup size alone doesn’t mean anything without the band. A 34C is a different cup volume than a 36C. That’s why our calculator asks for both your band and bust – to give you your exact cup letter for your specific band.

Cup Size Chart – Quick Reference

Here’s how cup letters match up to the difference between bust and band (in inches):

  • 0 – ½″ → AA
  • 1″ → A
  • 2″ → B
  • 3″ → C
  • 4″ → D
  • 5″ → DD / E
  • 6″ → DDD / F
  • 7″ → G
  • 8″ → H
  • and so on…

Note: US and UK cup letters match up to D. Beyond that, they differ – use our conversion tool for exact mapping.

Why Your Cup Size Might Change

Your cup size can change if your weight changes, if you’re pregnant or nursing, or even if you switch bra brands. That’s why it’s smart to measure again every few months. Our calculator gives you a fresh, reliable letter every time.

Sister Sizing – Same Cup Volume, Different Letter

If you know your band and cup, you can find sister sizes – different band/cup combos that hold the same volume. For example, a 34C has the same cup volume as a 32D and a 36B. If your band feels too tight, go up a band and down a cup; if it’s too loose, go down a band and up a cup. Our Sister Size Calculator can help you find these matches.
