Find the perfect sports bra size for running, yoga, gym, and everything in between – based on your activity level and body.

A sports bra is not the same as an everyday bra. It's designed to minimize breast movement during physical activity – and that changes how it should fit.

Why Sports Bras Fit Differently

Sports bras are engineered to reduce breast movement during physical activity. Unlike everyday bras, they typically have:

  • A firmer, tighter band – because most support comes from the band, not the straps
  • Less stretch in the fabric – to keep everything in place
  • Wider straps – to distribute weight and prevent digging
  • Different cup styles – compression (flattening) or encapsulation (separate cups)

Because of these differences, your sports bra size may be different from your everyday bra size. Many women need to size down in the band and up in the cup for high-impact activities.

Understanding Impact Levels

Sports bras are categorized by the level of support they provide:

🧘 Low Impact

Yoga, walking, pilates, stretching. Light compression, minimal bounce. Good for smaller busts or low-movement activities.

🚴 Medium Impact

Cycling, gym, hiking, dance. Moderate compression or encapsulation. Good for most everyday workouts.

🏃 High Impact

Running, jumping, HIIT, plyometrics. Maximum support with encapsulation or firm compression. Essential for large busts and high-bounce activities.

Your activity level determines the type of sports bra you need – and the size you should choose.

How to Measure for a Sports Bra

Measuring for a sports bra is similar to measuring for an everyday bra, but with a few key differences:

Step 1: Measure Your Underbust (Snug)

  • Wrap the tape around your ribcage, directly under your bust.
  • Pull it firmer than you would for an everyday bra – sports bras need a tight band for support.
  • Make sure the tape is level.
  • Write down this number.

Step 2: Measure Your Bust (Standing)

  • Measure around the fullest part of your chest – usually across the nipples.
  • Keep the tape loose – don't compress the tissue.
  • Write down this number.

Step 3: Calculate Your Band Size

  • For sports bras, use your snug underbust measurement directly – do not add 4-5 inches like you would for everyday bras.
  • Round to the nearest even number (e.g., 32" → 32 band).

Step 4: Calculate Your Cup Size

  • Subtract your band size from your bust measurement.
  • Each inch of difference equals one cup letter (1" = A, 2" = B, 3" = C, etc.).

For high impact, you may need to size up in the cup and size down in the band for maximum support.

Compression vs Encapsulation – Which Is Right for You?

  • Compression bras flatten the breasts against the chest. They work well for smaller busts and low-to-medium impact activities. They're simple, comfortable, and easy to wear.
  • Encapsulation bras have separate cups that hold each breast individually. They provide better support for larger busts and high-impact activities. They reduce bouncing and distribute weight more evenly.

Many high-impact sports bras combine both compression and encapsulation for maximum support.

Tips for Trying On Sports Bras

  • Test the band – It should be firm and not move when you move your arms. You should be able to fit two fingers under it comfortably.
  • Check the cups – No spillage at the top or sides. The center piece (gore) should lie flat against your chest.
  • Jump and move – Do a few jumping jacks or jog in place to test for bounce. If it feels unsupported, try a size down in the band or up in the cup.
  • Check the straps – They should be comfortable and not dig in. Adjustable straps are useful for different body shapes.
  • Try sister sizes – If the band feels too tight, try a sister size up (e.g., 34C → 36B). If it feels too loose, try a sister size down (e.g., 34C → 32D).

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I size up or down in a sports bra?

For high-impact sports bras, you may need to size down in the band and size up in the cup. For low-impact, stick to your regular size. Always try on different sizes to find the best fit.

What's the difference between compression and encapsulation?

Compression bras flatten the chest – good for smaller busts and low impact. Encapsulation bras have separate cups – better for larger busts and high impact.

Can I wear a sports bra for everyday use?

You can, but sports bras are designed for movement and may not provide the same shaping as everyday bras. For daily wear, choose a low-impact style for comfort.

How do I know if my sports bra is too small?

Signs include red marks that don't fade, spillage over the top, straps digging in, and difficulty breathing. If it feels uncomfortable, try a larger size or sister size.
